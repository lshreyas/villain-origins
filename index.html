THE CHINA QUESTION

Why isn't China the villain?

China shows up as the primary villain in just four of the films in our sample, even though it sat at the center of two decades of strategic anxiety. The chart below is the most likely reason. From the mid-2010s, China became Hollywood's largest single foreign market; films that wanted a release there had every reason not to write Chinese villains in the first place.

WHAT THE CHART SHOWS
A line that goes up, then back down

Hollywood's Chinese box office grew roughly 11× between 2010 and 2017, peaking at $3.4B. Across that same decade our dataset tags two films with an identifiably Chinese primary villain (Skyscraper, The Great Wall). Russia, with a fraction of the box office, gets about 10× as many villain roles.

WHAT WAS HAPPENING
Approval, not avoidance

China's film authority caps foreign imports and reviews each title for politically sensitive content. Studios that wanted access rewrote, recut, or never wrote Chinese antagonists. Red Dawn (2012) digitally swapped its Chinese villains for North Korean ones in post; World War Z moved the outbreak origin out of China; Top Gun: Maverick reportedly had wardrobe patches altered for the China cut.

WHAT TO WATCH
If China's Hollywood market keeps shrinking after 2024, the test is whether Chinese villains start showing up again.

Methodology

Hand-curated sample of well-known Hollywood films, 1940s through early 2025. Each film's primary antagonist is tagged by country of origin, or Non-national for aliens, AI, monsters, and internal demons.

How to use

I. hover or click streams to drill into the films. II. hover dots for film details. III. swap decades to redraw the map. IV. read top-to-bottom as a hierarchy. ESC resets the streamgraph.

Caveats

The sample is iconic, not exhaustive. Some characters defy a single flag (Le Chiffre, Keyser Söze, Loki); we place them where most viewers locate them. Regional categories like Middle East and Latin America collapse multiple national origins.
